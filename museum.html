Route

How content becomes a museum record

01 · Capture ends — The content becomes a record

The master, transcript, shot list, releases, and supporting papers are treated as one evidence package — not a folder of loose files.

02 · Review completes — Integrity, facts, and clearance are tested

Checksums are verified, names and dates are checked, public cuts are separated from restricted masters, and every exclusion is logged.

03 · Accession closes — The file receives a permanent number

A MET-YYYY-CLS-NNNN reference names the holding, its class, its registrar, its rights position, and its open exceptions.

04 · The museum opens — Only the cleared public label is exhibited

Visitors see the citation, context, and permitted-use summary. Restricted masters and private identifiers stay behind the wall.

Reading a label

What every exhibit must disclose

  1. Accession reference — the permanent name of the record.
  2. Creator and depositor — who made it and who had authority to place it.
  3. Capture and verification dates — when it entered, and when it was checked.
  4. Rights summary — what may be done with it, in plain sight.
  5. Restriction note — what is withheld, and why.
  6. Citation — the exact string others reproduce when they reference it.

Museum rules

  • No unpublished, restricted, or contested file is exhibited as a finished holding.
  • The public label never exposes tax identifiers, government identifiers, private contact details, or unreleased masters.
  • A shot list, transcript, release, or review memo can be an exhibit when it explains the life of the work.
  • Corrections are dated addenda. The museum does not silently rewrite the record.
  • Every exhibit links back to the Handbook gates that admitted it.
